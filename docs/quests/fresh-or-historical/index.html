Lesson 4 of 5 · 5 min · Data quality

Fresh or historical?

Find out when a reading stops counting as current, and why the station cares.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Press Ask for a reading and look at the line under the numbers.
  2. Wait and watch. When does Fresh change to Historical?
  3. Pause contact. What does the line say now?

Check your work

You found how many seconds a reading stays fresh.

Talk it over

Why is old information sometimes worse than no information?

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Get a reading and record the age at which the label changes to Historical.
  2. Repeat with contact paused and compare.
  3. Write one rule the next crew can use to decide whether a reading is current.

Check your work

Your rule names the 5 second limit and what happens while contact is paused.

Talk it over

Would a 5 second limit work for a weather balloon logging once a minute? What would you choose?

Steps for professional crews

  1. Get a reading and compare captured_unix_ms in Raw telemetry with the moment the label changes.
  2. Confirm the threshold matches MAX_AGE in the station source (5000 ms).
  3. Read Sample::age_ms in src/main.rs and describe what the station does with a sample whose capture time is ahead of the receiver clock.

Check your work

You confirmed the threshold and explained the future-timestamp rejection.

Talk it over

How would you add clock uncertainty to the sample format so the ground can reason about it?
