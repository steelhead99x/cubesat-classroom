Lesson 2 of 11 · 5 min · Data return

Bring back a picture

Start the test camera and count how many frames reach the ground.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Press Take pictures.
  2. Count the pictures as they arrive. Watch the white square move across the colours.
  3. When it stops, write down how many pictures arrived out of 8.

Check your work

You wrote down a number out of 8 and where the white square stopped.

Talk it over

Why is it useful that the camera sends lots of small pictures instead of one big one?

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Press Start video and time the burst from the first frame to the last.
  2. Record the frame count, the burst duration and the time between frames.
  3. Sketch the position of the marker in the first and last frames.

Check your work

You recorded 8 frames, the total time and the interval between frames.

Talk it over

If each frame were 20 KB, how many kilobytes per second does this burst need?

Steps for professional crews

  1. Start a video request. The reply is one MoQ group on exchange.v1 with a JSON header frame, then 8 JPEG frames at 500 ms.
  2. Note the estimated size of each frame and the total bytes listed in the log when the group completes.
  3. Estimate the average bit rate of the burst in kbit/s.

Check your work

You have per-frame sizes, the group total and a bit-rate estimate with your working.

Talk it over

Each frame is independently decodable. Compare that with H.264 inter-frame video over a lossy link.
