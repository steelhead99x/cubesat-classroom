Lesson 10 of 11 · 10 min · Radio rules

Who may transmit?

Use the rule finder on cubesat.app to see how the same 915 MHz radio can be allowed, need a licence, or not be allowed.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Open Bands and find Which rule am I under?.
  2. Pick the first radio in the list. Look at the cards below it. Which say Fits?
  3. Turn on Licensed operator in charge. Does the amateur radio card fit now? Read its reasons to find out what else it needs, then try that too.

Check your work

You wrote down one rule that fits and one that does not for the same radio, and the two switches the amateur card needed.

Talk it over

Radio waves are shared by everyone nearby. Why do we need rules about who may send?

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Pick Licensed ham, 125 kHz, open messages. Turn on Messages encrypted and see what happens to the amateur radio rule.
  2. Pick Wi-Fi HaLow, 1 MHz channel. Which rule does it fit, and what does the card say this project uses it for?
  3. Find a card marked Unverified. Write down the measurement it asks for.

Check your work

You explained why encryption changes the amateur result and named the measurement one card needs.

Talk it over

Students may listen to any band, but a licensed operator must be in charge to transmit on amateur bands. Why is listening treated so differently from sending?

Steps for professional crews

  1. Read findRules in the cubesat.app repository (mobile/src/lib/ruleFinder.ts) and open the rule sections each card cites.
  2. Choose one preset and trace, step by step, why each card gives its result.
  3. Find one assumption the rule finder makes that a real radio could break, such as antenna gain or duty cycle.

Check your work

Your trace cites the rule section for each step, and you named one assumption with the measurement that would test it.

Talk it over

The app says unverified instead of guessing when a measurement is missing. When should a tool refuse to give an answer?
