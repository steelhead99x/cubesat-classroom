Lesson 1 of 11 · 5 min · Measurement and units

Make first contact

Ask the practice CubeSat for a sensor reading and record it with its units.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Press Ask for a reading.
  2. Watch the mission log. Your question goes up to the satellite, and its answer comes back down.
  3. Write down the three numbers you get. Copy the little letters after each number too, like °C or V.

Check your work

You have three numbers, and each one has its unit next to it.

Talk it over

If a friend wrote "temperature 20" with no unit, what might you guess wrong?

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Press Get sensor reading and note the time the command was sent.
  2. Record temperature (°C), bus voltage (V) and light (lx), plus the reply time in milliseconds from the log.
  3. Take two more readings. Is the reply time always the same?

Check your work

Your table has three readings, each with units and a reply time in ms.

Talk it over

What could make one reply slower than another on a real radio link?

Steps for professional crews

  1. Send a sensors request. The log shows the command published on the classroom/commands broadcast and the reply group on exchange.v1.
  2. Open Raw telemetry and record the sequence, captured_unix_ms and source fields along with the three values.
  3. Take three readings and compute the mean round-trip time and its spread.

Check your work

You logged three JSON samples with sequence numbers and a mean RTT with its range.

Talk it over

The reply carries a capture timestamp from the payload clock. What has to be true about the two clocks before you trust an age calculation?
