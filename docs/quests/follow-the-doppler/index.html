Lesson 8 of 11 · 15 min · Tuning and Doppler shift

Follow the Doppler shift

Tune the CSC-R1P receiver pedal, then keep a passing satellite's signal in the passband as its frequency drifts.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Do the three pedal tasks shown above the pedal. Tap ENGAGE, tune to 145.800 MHz, then set GAIN and LEVEL.
  2. Write down the frequency, gain and level that worked.
  3. Answer the four Predict, then test questions under the pedal, then try each one. The trainer tells you if your prediction was right. Watch the row of stages. A red stage is where the signal is stopped.
  4. Check the ENGAGE light is on, then press Start a pass. When the satellite rises, turn TUNE to keep the readout saying carrier heard. The score only counts time with the pedal engaged.

Check your work

You have the three settings written down and the percent of the pass you heard, from the result line.

Talk it over

The satellite's signal seems to change frequency as it goes past. Have you heard a siren change pitch as it drives by?

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Before each pass, check the ENGAGE light. If it is off, tap ENGAGE. The score only counts seconds while the pedal is engaged, so a muted pass scores 0%. Restart turns ENGAGE off again.
  2. Set the TUNE step to 5 kHz before starting the first pass. Record the percent heard.
  3. Use the TUNE step button to select 1 kHz, then run another pass. Record the percent heard again.
  4. During the second pass, write down the Doppler value at rise, at the highest point and at set.
  5. After each pass, look at the chart under the result. The gold line is the satellite and the green band is what you could hear. Write down where the gold line left the green band.

Check your work

You have two scores, one for each step size, three Doppler values with units, and where on the pass you lost the carrier.

Talk it over

The receiver hears signals within 1.5 kHz of where it is tuned. Using that, explain why 5 kHz steps miss the start of the pass.

Steps for professional crews

  1. Estimate the largest Doppler shift with Δf = f × v / c, using 145.8 MHz and the satellite's speed along your line of sight. Compare with the readout at rise.
  2. Read makePass in site/src/lib/pass.mjs. Find where it decides a carrier is heard and where it marks a reading as decoded.
  3. Run cargo run --locked -- receiver-sim --track-doppler --seconds 660 and again without --track-doppler. Compare how many snapshots say acquired.

Check your work

Your estimate is within about 10% of the readout, and you can name the two conditions for decoded and the difference Doppler tracking makes.

Talk it over

On 70 cm the shift is about three times bigger. What would you change on the pedal, or in software, to follow it?
