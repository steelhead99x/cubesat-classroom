Lesson 6 of 11 · 15 min · Tracking and radio links

Catch the Pass

Point a ground antenna at a passing satellite and choose a radio setting that still works when it is low.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Open the game and play level 1, First light. Keep the circle on the satellite. The signal bar turns green when you are catching packets.
  2. Write down your packets and stars. Earning at least one star opens the next level. A locked level says locked under its name.
  3. Play level 2, Low pass. The satellite stays close to the ground. Try Fast, then try Far. Which one caught more?

Check your work

You have two scores written down, one for each level, and you know which radio setting worked better on the low pass.

Talk it over

The satellite is farther away when it is low in the sky. Why would that make its signal weaker?

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Levels open in order. Earn at least one star on a level to open the next. If Low pass or Into the shadow says locked, play the levels before it first. The game remembers stars in this browser, so a new browser or a private window starts with only level 1 open.
  2. Play Low pass twice. Use Fast the whole time the first run and Far the whole time the second. Record packets caught each time.
  3. Play level 4, Into the shadow. Watch the battery after the satellite enters Earth's shadow. Use Sending to let it rest when the charge gets low.
  4. Make a table of level, radio setting, packets caught and lowest battery percent.

Check your work

Your table has at least three runs, and you can say in one sentence when Far beats Fast.

Talk it over

Far reaches weaker signals but each packet takes longer to send. What does a slow packet cost a satellite that is in the shadow?

Steps for professional crews

  1. Read the SF table and packetEnergy in site/src/games/catch-the-pass/logic.mjs. Fast, Middle and Far are LoRa spreading factors 7, 9 and 12.
  2. From minSnr, work out how many dB of extra margin Far gives over Fast. From airtime, work out how many times more energy each Far packet costs.
  3. Play Low pass (earn a star on First light first if it is locked) and, near the horizon, switch settings and watch which one the signal bar can still decode. Compare what you see with your numbers.

Check your work

You have the margin in dB and the energy ratio, with working, and one observation from the game that agrees or disagrees with them.

Talk it over

The game shortens SF12 airtime so a pass fits in a minute. Which of your conclusions would change with real airtime, and which would not?
