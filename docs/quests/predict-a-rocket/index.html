Lesson 11 of 11 · 20 min · Modelling and measurement

Predict a rocket flight

Enter what your crew measured, pick a certified motor, and get a prediction to test at a club launch.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Open the workbook. Leave the bench-test numbers as they are.
  2. Pick a motor in part 1. Look at the prediction card for it.
  3. Write down the predicted highest point (apogee) in metres and whether each check passed.

Check your work

You have one apogee with its unit and a list of which checks passed.

Talk it over

The workbook makes a guess before the flight. Why is it useful to guess first and measure later?

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Compare the prediction cards for two motors. Record apogee, stability and rail exit speed for each.
  2. In part 3, add 20 g to the payload mass and see how the apogee changes.
  3. Write which motor you would ask the club's safety officer about, and why.

Check your work

Your table compares two motors with units, and you measured the effect of the 20 g change.

Talk it over

Stability compares where the weight balances with where the air pushes. What happens if those two points get too close?

Steps for professional crews

  1. Read what the workbook says about its model (point mass, simple drag, rail friction). List what it leaves out.
  2. In part 2, change the friction numbers by ±20% and record how rail exit speed changes. In part 4, try three wind speeds and record what the checks say.
  3. After a real club flight, enter the altimeter's apogee in Flights and compute the percent error against the prediction.

Check your work

You have a list of what the model leaves out, the effect of the friction and wind changes, and, if you flew, the percent error labelled as measured.

Talk it over

One motor card is marked MADE UP, because it is a test curve and not a real motor. Why would a prediction from it be useless at a real launch, even if the code is right?
