Lesson 5 of 11 · 10 min · Patterns in data

Light and shadow

Take readings over a minute and explain why the light level sometimes drops to zero.

Pick your level

On paper, write the lesson title and your level. Number your notes to match the steps. Tick each one when you finish it.

Steps for beginner crews

  1. Make a table with two columns, time and light.Copy link · copied
  2. Ask for a reading every 10 seconds for one minute.Copy link · copied
  3. Circle the rows where light is 0 lx.Copy link · copied

Check your work

Your table has at least six rows, and you circled the zeros.

Copy link · copied

Talk it over

Where could a satellite go that would make its light sensor read zero?

Copy link · copied

Steps for intermediate crews

  1. Record time, light (lx) and temperature (°C) every 5 seconds for one minute.Copy link · copied
  2. Find how long the light stays on and how long it stays off.Copy link · copied
  3. Plot light against time on paper or in a spreadsheet.Copy link · copied

Check your work

You measured the on and off durations and drew the pattern.

Copy link · copied

Talk it over

The simulated cycle is 30 s. A real low-orbit pass around Earth takes about 90 minutes. What changes?

Copy link · copied

Steps for professional crews

  1. Sample once per second for 40 s and record sequence, light and temperature.Copy link · copied
  2. Derive the light rule from the sequence numbers and confirm it against Sample::synthetic in the station source. In this demo the sequence is the time in seconds, like the station's simulate command; the installed classroom station counts requests instead.Copy link · copied
  3. Describe how you would replace the synthetic eclipse with one computed from an orbit model.Copy link · copied

Check your work

You stated the rule (lit when sequence mod 30 is below 20) and verified it against the data.

Copy link · copied

Talk it over

What inputs would an eclipse model need, and where would you get them for a real satellite?
