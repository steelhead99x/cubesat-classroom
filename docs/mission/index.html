Flight preparation

A classroom experiment on a research spaceflight

What would a small sensor kit record on a suborbital research flight? This page helps a class predict it.

Read the flight plan

An independent volunteer project. Flight details come from public sources. The flight host decides what flies and when.

The ride, minute by minute

About 70 minutes from take-off to landing. Most of it is a slow climb. The part in space lasts a few minutes, so the kit has to be recording before it starts.

Inside the cabin the air stays at about the pressure of a mountain town. A pressure sensor there will not show the near-vacuum outside.

What the kit measures

A small printed CubeSat with an Arduino, a rechargeable battery and a clock that time-stamps every reading.

  • Temperature
  • Pressure
  • Acceleration
  • Rotation
  • Magnetic field
  • Carbon dioxide
  • Humidity
  • Light

Seven steps toward a flight

Each step ends with something a reviewer can check. Following the public guide does not earn a place on the flight; the host decides.

  1. 01
    Predict

    Run the flight model and write down what each sensor should record.

  2. 02
    Bench

    Log for the full length of a flight on a desk, with a deliberate gap.

  3. 03
    Compare

    Explain the same sensors on a glider, a spaceship and the ISS.

  4. 04
    Package

    Draft the payload paperwork and list every open question.

  5. 05
    Host review

    Get written answers from the flight host.

  6. 06
    Rehearse

    Time the switch-on, logging, switch-off and data hand-back.

  7. 07
    Fly and share

    Publish the measurements next to the predictions.
