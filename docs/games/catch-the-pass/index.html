      How to play

      7 steps. Each one has its own link to share.

      1. Step 1Copy link · Copied

        Pick a level

        Start with level 1. A star on a level unlocks the next one. Easy mode, on at first, makes passes slower and the antenna circle bigger.

        Controls: 1 First lightEasy mode

      2. Step 2Copy link · Copied

        Press Start

        Press Start, or the Enter key. After a short countdown the satellite rises on the left.

        Controls: Start

      3. Step 3Copy link · Copied

        Keep the circle on the satellite

        Press or hold ▲ ◀ ▶ ▼, drag on the sky, or use the arrow keys. When the signal bar turns green, you are catching packets.

        Controls: ▲◀▶▼

      4. Step 4Copy link · Copied

        Pick a radio setting

        Fast catches packets quickly when the satellite is high and close. Far still works when it is low and far away. Keys 1, 2 and 3 work too.

        Controls: FastMiddleFar

      5. Step 5Copy link · Copied

        Look after the battery

        In Earth’s shadow every packet uses stored energy. If the battery runs low, press Sending (or Space) to let the satellite rest.

        Controls: Sending

      6. Step 6Copy link · Copied

        Tune the receiver

        Keep the receiver pedal engaged and tuned to 915 MHz. Lower the gain if the input meter turns red.

        Controls: EngagedTuneGain

      7. Step 7Copy link · Copied

        Earn stars

        Catch enough packets before the satellite sets to earn 1, 2 or 3 stars.

      Keyboard: Arrow keys or W A S D move the antenna. 1, 2 and 3 pick the radio setting. Space switches sending. Enter starts. P or Esc pauses.

      What is real here

      A ground station must point its antenna at a CubeSat in low Earth orbit for the few minutes it is overhead. Low in the sky the satellite is up to five times farther away, so its downlink is weaker.

      Many CubeSats send telemetry with LoRa radios at 915 MHz. Each spreading factor really does decode at a different signal-to-noise ratio: SF7 needs about −7.5 dB and SF12 works down to −20 dB, but each SF12 packet takes about 30 times longer to send. The ISM band is shared with other devices, which is the noise you have to avoid.

      The live sky sends each station's antenna and packets over Media over QUIC (MoQ), the same relay technology the classroom plans to use to share real satellite passes between schools.

      The game adapts the CSC-R1 pedal controls for a simulated 915 MHz LoRa receiver. The physical CSC-R1 receiver is designed for 2 m and 70 cm. Passes and packet airtimes are shortened for play.
