      How to play

      • Move the antenna: tap or hold the ▲ ◀ ▶ ▼ buttons, drag on the sky with a finger, move the mouse over the sky, or use the arrow keys or W A S D.
      • Catch packets: keep the circle on the satellite. When the signal bar turns green, you are catching packets and earning credits.
      • Set up your receiver: choose a wide beam for easier aiming or a narrow beam for more gain. Keep the pedal engaged and tuned to 915 MHz.
      • Radio setting: 🐇 Fast catches packets quickly when the satellite is close. 🐢 Far works when it is low and far away. Keys 1, 2 and 3 work too.
      • Sending: press Space or the 📡 button to let the satellite rest and save its battery.
      • Pause: P or Esc. Start: Enter. Sound starts off. Easy mode (on at first) makes passes slower and the antenna’s circle bigger.
      • Earn 1 to 3 stars per pass. A star on a level unlocks the next one.

      What is real here

      A ground station must point its antenna at a CubeSat in low Earth orbit for the few minutes it is overhead. Low in the sky the satellite is up to five times farther away, so its downlink is weaker.

      Many CubeSats send telemetry with LoRa radios at 915 MHz. Each spreading factor really does decode at a different signal-to-noise ratio: SF7 needs about −7.5 dB and SF12 works down to −20 dB, but each SF12 packet takes about 30 times longer to send. The ISM band is shared with other devices, which is the noise you have to avoid.

      The live sky sends each station's antenna and packets over Media over QUIC (MoQ), the same relay technology the classroom plans to use to share real satellite passes between schools.

      The game adapts the CSC-R1 pedal controls for a simulated 915 MHz LoRa receiver. The physical CSC-R1 receiver is designed for 2 m and 70 cm. Passes and packet airtimes are shortened for play.
