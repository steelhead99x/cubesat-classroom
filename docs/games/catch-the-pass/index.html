      How to play

      • Aim: move the mouse over the sky, drag with a finger, or click the sky and use the arrow keys or W A S D. The antenna turns at a limited speed, just like a real rotator.
      • Spreading factor: press 1, 2 or 3 (or tap SF7, SF9, SF12). The line on the signal bar is the weakest signal that SF can hear.
      • Transmitter: press Space to tell the satellite to hold or resume sending. Holding saves battery.
      • Pause: P or Esc. Start: Enter. Sound starts off.
      • Earn 1 to 3 stars per pass. A star on a level unlocks the next one.

      What is real here

      A ground station must point its antenna at a CubeSat in low Earth orbit for the few minutes it is overhead. Low in the sky the satellite is up to five times farther away, so its downlink is weaker.

      Many CubeSats send telemetry with LoRa radios at 915 MHz. Each spreading factor really does decode at a different signal-to-noise ratio: SF7 needs about −7.5 dB and SF12 works down to −20 dB, but each SF12 packet takes about 30 times longer to send. The ISM band is shared with other devices, which is the noise you have to avoid.

      The live sky sends each station's antenna and packets over Media over QUIC (MoQ), the same relay technology the classroom plans to use to share real satellite passes between schools.

      Simplified for play: passes are sped up about 15 times, SF9 and SF12 packets are shortened, and the sky view flattens the dome into a rectangle.
