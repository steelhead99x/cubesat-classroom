---
title: Predict a rocket flight
patch: Apogee guess
order: 11
summary: Enter what your crew measured, pick a certified motor, and get a prediction to test at a club launch.
minutes: 20
skill: Modelling and measurement
tool:
  name: Rocket flight workbook
  href: /rockoon/workbook/
  note: For older learners with an adult. Predictions are a desk simulation. Model rockets fly only at a club launch, under its safety officer.
beginner:
  steps:
    - Open the workbook. Leave the bench-test numbers as they are.
    - Pick a motor in part 1. Look at the prediction card for it.
    - Write down the predicted highest point (apogee) in metres and whether each check passed.
  check: You have one apogee with its unit and a list of which checks passed.
  discuss: The workbook makes a guess before the flight. Why is it useful to guess first and measure later?
intermediate:
  steps:
    - Compare the prediction cards for two motors. Record apogee, stability and rail exit speed for each.
    - In part 3, add 20 g to the payload mass and see how the apogee changes.
    - Write which motor you would ask the club's safety officer about, and why.
  check: Your table compares two motors with units, and you measured the effect of the 20 g change.
  discuss: Stability compares where the weight balances with where the air pushes. What happens if those two points get too close?
professional:
  steps:
    - Read what the workbook says about its model (point mass, simple drag, rail friction). List what it leaves out.
    - In part 2, change the friction numbers by ±20% and record how rail exit speed changes. In part 4, try three wind speeds and record what the checks say.
    - After a real club flight, enter the altimeter's apogee in **Flights** and compute the percent error against the prediction.
  check: You have a list of what the model leaves out, the effect of the friction and wind changes, and, if you flew, the percent error labelled as measured.
  discuss: One motor card is marked MADE UP, because it is a test curve and not a real motor. Why would a prediction from it be useless at a real launch, even if the code is right?
---
