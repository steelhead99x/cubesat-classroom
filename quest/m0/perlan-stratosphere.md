# [S] Fly with Perlan 2: the stratosphere without an engine

## Goal

Learn how the Perlan 2 glider carried student CubeSat experiments to the stratosphere, and predict what a sensor kit would record there.

## Plan

### Background

Perlan 2 is a two-seat research glider with no engine. It rides mountain waves, rising air behind the Andes that is strengthened by the polar vortex. A tow plane releases it at about 42,000–45,000 ft. On September 2, 2018 it reached 76,124 ft over El Calafate, Argentina, a record for gliders. The cockpit is pressurised. The science bay is not: it holds four CubeSat frames and lets outside air flow past them. Teachers in Space flew student experiments on Perlan 2 in 2015–2016 and again in 2022–2023.

Sources: [Perlan Project and Teachers in Space](https://perlanproject.org/teachers-space-visit-minden/), [Teachers in Space history](https://www.tis.org/what-we-do), [record flight report](https://aopa.org/news-and-media/all-news/2018/september/04/high-flying-perlan-2-sailplane-makes-history), [student experiment announcement](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/student-stem-experiments-edge-space-142158082.html).

### Steps

1. Find El Calafate and the Andes on a map. Mark which way the wind must blow to make waves behind the mountains.
2. The science bay is open to outside air. Predict what pressure and temperature do between the ground and 76,000 ft. Air pressure there is roughly 3% of sea level.
3. Run the balloon bench log from the [balloon lab](/docs/balloon-lab.md) and compare its shape with your prediction. A balloon and the Perlan bay both sit in outside air.
4. Compare with a real record: open a high-altitude data set from the [Teachers in Space flight-data archive](https://www.tis.org/flightdata). Note what flew, when, and the units.
5. Check your work: your prediction should show pressure falling the whole way up, and temperature falling and then levelling off. Say where your guess differed from the archive data.
6. Share the crew nickname, the prediction and one question you would ask a Perlan pilot.

### Levels

- **Beginner:** explain how a glider can climb without an engine.
- **Intermediate:** convert 76,124 ft to kilometres and compare it with a passenger jet and with the spaceship's 87.5 km.
- **Professional:** the bay's air intake means sensors see moving air. Explain how airflow could change a temperature reading and how you would test for it on the bench.
