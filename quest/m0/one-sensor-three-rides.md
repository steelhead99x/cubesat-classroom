# [S] One sensor, three rides

## Goal

Explain why the same pressure and temperature sensors tell three different stories on three vehicles: the Perlan 2 glider, a suborbital spaceship and the International Space Station.

## Plan

1. Fill in this table. Use the linked guides, and write "not sure" where you can't find a source.

   | | Perlan 2 science bay | Spaceship cabin | ISS cabin |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | How high? | | | |

   | Is the sensor in outside air? | | | |

2. Sources: [the Perlan 2 quest](/quest/m0/perlan-stratosphere.md), [the suborbital flight plan](/docs/suborbital-flight-plan.md), [the ISS orbit quest](/quest/m0/iss-orbit.md) and the [balloon lab](/docs/balloon-lab.md).
3. Sketch the pressure reading against time for each ride on one page.
4. Check your work: one of the three sketches should fall to a few percent of sea-level pressure. The other two should stay nearly flat. Explain which and why.
5. Share the crew nickname, the table and the sketches. Label them as predictions, not measurements.

## Levels

- **Beginner:** say which ride is the highest, which is the longest and which has outside air on the sensor.
- **Intermediate:** explain why astronauts float on the ISS for months but spaceship passengers float for about three minutes.
- **Professional:** compare speeds. The spaceship model reaches about 0.95 km/s; the ISS travels near 7.7 km/s. Estimate how much more energy per kilogram orbit needs.
