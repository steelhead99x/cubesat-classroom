# [S] Track a pet by its collar

## Goal

Learn how a tracking collar tells an owner where a pet is, then find a hidden "pet" in your classroom from its collar readings. Label classroom results as a hardware bench or desk simulation.

## Background

A pet tracker is a small tag on a collar. It listens to GPS satellites to work out where it is, then sends that position to the owner's phone. Scientists use the same idea on wild animals: Yellowstone's wolves wear GPS collars that report by satellite. [Yellowstone wolf management](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/management/wolf.htm)

The collar is like a tiny CubeSat on a pet: sensors, a clock, a battery and a radio.

## Plan

1. Choose a toy animal as the class pet and give it a name.
2. One group hides the pet with its "collar": a classroom sensor board, or a card with made-up readings if you have no hardware. Every few minutes they write a clue card with the time, the light level (bright or dark) and the temperature (warm or cool).
3. The tracking group reads the clue cards in order and marks on a room map where the pet could be: near the window, by the heater, in a cupboard.
4. After four clues, the trackers guess the spot. Then the hiders show it.
5. Check your work: every clue has a time and a unit or label, and your map shows how each clue narrowed the search.
6. Share the crew nickname, the room map and which clue helped most.

## Try it with your own pet

Ask a trusted adult first. For one afternoon, write down where your pet is every 30 minutes using words, not addresses: "sunny windowsill", "under the bed", "garden". Bring the list to class and draw a chart of your pet's favourite places.

Keep it private: a real tracker's map shows where you live. Don't share the map, your address or photos of your home.

## Talk it over

A collar's battery has to last. Would you send a position every minute or twice a day? What do you lose each way?
