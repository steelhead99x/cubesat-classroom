# [S] Wool, fibre and static: what should cover the kit?

## Goal

Find out by testing which fabric makes a safe cover for a sensor kit. Compare wool and other natural fibres with plastic ones. Label the result a hardware bench test.

## Plan

### Background

Wool is a sustainable fibre: sheep grow a new fleece every year, and wool is sheared, washed, combed, spun into yarn and woven or felted into cloth. It cushions well and does not catch fire easily.

Static is the catch. Rubbing some materials together builds up an electric charge, and a spark too small to feel can damage electronics. Wool builds charge easily when the air is dry and holds less when the air is damp, because the fibre soaks up moisture. So "is wool a good cover?" is a real question with a testable answer.

Electronics makers protect boards with **anti-static bags** and fabrics that have conducting threads woven in. Use those as the comparison.

### Steps

1. Collect samples about the size of your hand: wool felt, cotton, a plastic fabric such as polyester fleece, and an anti-static bag. Do not test on the kit itself.
2. Tear a tissue into rice-sized bits. Rub one sample ten times on a plastic ruler, then hold the ruler just above the bits. Count how many jump up.
3. Repeat for each sample, three times each. Use the same ruler, the same ten rubs and the same height.
4. Write down the room's humidity if you have a meter, or the weather (dry or damp day).
5. Check your work: a table with four materials, three counts each, an average for each, and the humidity or weather.
6. Decide: which material would you put next to the electronics, and which would you use as padding around the outside? Share the crew nickname, your table and your choice.

### Buying a cover

- Next to the electronics: an anti-static bag. They cost little and are made for this.
- Around the outside: padding your test supports, such as wool felt, if it scored well in your room.
- For a flight, the host decides which materials may go on board. Bring your test table to that conversation.

### Talk it over

Would your result be the same on a dry winter day, or in the dry air of an aircraft cabin? How could you test that?
